What the score is based on

The base is the Nutri-Score 2023 scales: sugar, salt, saturated fat, calories, protein, fibre and the share of fruit, vegetables and legumes per 100 g. Independent experts in nutrition and public health updated this algorithm for seven European countries.

Large EPIC studies (hundreds of thousands of people across Europe) link diets with more foods of a worse Nutri-Score to a higher risk of cancer and cardiovascular disease.

On top of that, we look at the ingredients — only where there is an official position or strong evidence:

  • Sugar, salt and fatsWHO guidelines: less free sugar and salt, and unsaturated fats in place of saturated ones.
  • Whole grains and fibreA review in The Lancet (2019): 185 cohort studies and 58 clinical trials.
  • NitritesIARC (WHO): processed meat is a Group 1 carcinogen. EFSA (2023): nitrosamines in food raise a health concern.
  • Trans fatsWHO and EU Regulation 2019/649, which limits industrial trans fats.
  • SweetenersWHO (2023) advises against them for weight control.
  • Colours and E171EU Regulation 1333/2008 requires a warning on the label for some colours. E171 has been banned in the EU since 2022 after EFSA’s opinion.

Lichi’s 0–10 score is its own method built on this base, not Nutri-Score. Lichi shows the official Nutri-Score A–E label separately when Open Food Facts has it. An E number on its own does not lower the score.
